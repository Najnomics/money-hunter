# #98039 [LOW]

Source: https://reports.immunefi.com/plether/98039-sc-low-committed-open-orders-can-be-cancelled-at-no-cost-after-their-fill-price-is-known-because-any-caller-can-shrink-housepool-depth-through-orderrouter-settlelpepoch-incomplete-fix-of-sc-m4-c

Brief/Intro
At commit a82d4fefb02768e5b3bddcc97dee371eee24fea8 an open order is re-planned at execution time against the live HousePool.totalAssets() , and the planner's skew cap maxSkewAssets = netDepth * maxSkewRatio reserves nothing for orders that are committed but not yet executed. OrderRouter.settleLpEpoch is permissionless and funds any matured LP redemption out of the pool, so any caller can lower totalAssets() by the matured amount at a moment of its choosing. A SKEW_TOO_HIGH or SOLVENCY_EXCEEDED result at execution is classified ProtocolStateInvalidated and terminalizes the order as PlannerRejected with the full margin released and, for a self-executing account, the bounty refunded. The consequence is that a trader who has committed an order sized near the cap, and who has (or who merely finds) a matured, not yet funded LP redemption, can wait until the order's fill tick and the ticks after it are public, then decide whether that order fills or is cancelled for free. SECURITY.md promises LPs that "binding order semantics and the lack of a user cancellation path prevent traders from turning queued intents into free options"; the fix shipped for the pre-audit finding SC-M4 (plether-core PR 24) changed only the oracle pricing rule and left this third component of SC-M4 intact. The value of the option is bounded by how far the basket moves inside the few seconds that honest keepers leave between commit and execution, so the pool does not lose value in the ordinary case; it fails to deliver the binding-order guarantee it promises, and in a fast market it pays a stale fill against LPs with no offsetting losing leg. The attached Foundry proof executes the whole cycle through public entrypoints and measures the economics at the release risk parameters.

6. Why this is an incomplete fix rather than a known issue
The pre-audit finding SC-M4 ("Risk Free Option in 60Sec Oracle Window") described three components: the 60 s keeper tick choice, the dual-order tick selection, and "Component 3: The LP Order Cancellation. When a price move is too large, the attacker can trigger a full margin refund on the losing order by manipulating the vault depth ... Executing the losing order then triggers SOLVENCY_EXCEEDED due to reduced vault depth. This propagates as ProtocolStateInvalidated, granting a full margin return." The team's response was "Fixed in Plether-Fi/plether-core#24". That PR replaced the keeper's tick choice with the unique-first-tick rule (components 1 and 2). Nothing in sections 1 to 5 above was changed: execution still re-plans on live depth, the cap still has no reservation for committed orders, the depth codes are still terminal with a full refund, and settleLpEpoch is still permissionless. Component 3 is live at the pinned commit, and SECURITY.md still states the guarantee it breaks:

7. Attack sequence (as executed by the proof)
