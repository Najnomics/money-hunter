## About Immune fi

Immunefi is the security platform for the onchain economy, and 93% of crypto's critical vulnerability disclosures now flow through it. Founded in December 2020, it protects more than $180 billion in assets across 650-plus protocols, including MakerDAO, Optimism, Filecoin, LayerZero, Chainlink, Arbitrum, Lido, and Polygon. It has helped prevent more than $25 billion in hacks and paid more than $140 million in bounties, more than the rest of the market combined. Its unified security platform combines bug bounties, audit competitions, AI-assisted security, and coordinated vulnerability disclosure, powered by more than 85,000 researchers and a proprietary exploit dataset built since 2020. The thesis: crowdsourced security, supercharged with AI, is the only defense that scales to meet AI-driven attacks. Learn how you can benefit too at immunefi.com.

## Immune fi Audit Competitions

Immunefi has one of the largest communities of security researchers, with over 85,000 members. This 
community is mobilised through Audit Competitions. 
In audit competitions, a prize pool is shared, and the codebase is opened to Immunefi's researcher network 
for a fixed competition window. Audit Competitions are quantitatively superior to private audits, and these 
findings are supported by data from 1,178 public audit reports from Trail of Bits, Zellic, and OpenZeppelin. 
1. Improving the cost efficiency of bug finding: On average, a critical or high vulnerability costs about 
$66,000 in a private audit. This cost is reduced by about 10x in an audit competition, with an average of 
$6,548. 
2. Improving coverage, scrutiny and findings: An audit assigns 2 to 5 people to your code, while recent 
Immunefi competitions drew 952, 943 and 1,415 submissions. Hundreds of researchers cover more of the 
codebase and find more bugs. 
The median Immunefi audit competition finds 2 serious findings, compared to 0 for a Tier-1 private audit. The 
same applies for critical bugs: more than 1 in 2 Immunefi audit competitions find those, compared to only 1 in 
6 Tier-1 audits. All in all, Immunefi audit competitions average 6.2 critical or high findings, and Tier-1 audits 
only about 1.5.

## Terminology

For the purpose of this assessment we adopt the Immunefi severity classification defined by the Plether Audit Competition programme. Severity is determined by impact: each finding is mapped to a named impact from the programme's Impacts in Scope table, and the severity of that impact is the severity of the finding.

A runnable proof of concept was required for every submission at every severity level. Insight is a tier below Low, used for observations with no demonstrated in-scope impact; it cannot be self-selected at submission and is designated by Immunefi or the project team.

The band below is the one that applies to the assets in scope of this competition.

Insights aren’t included in the table, since they lack severity/impact.

IMM-SC-CRITICAL-97591 
Junior NAV ignores open positions' signed vpiAccrued, letting a trader who is also an 
LP extract incumbent principal 

| Report ID | #97591 |
| --- | --- |
| Severity | Critical |
| Category | Smart Contract |
| Asset | https://github.com/immunefi-team/audit-comp-plether/blob/ a82d4fefb02768e5b3bddcc97dee371eee24fea8/packages/perps/src/ MarginClearinghouse.sol |
| Impact | Direct theft of any user funds, whether at-rest or in-motion, other than unclaimed yield |

Description

Plether Perps prices Junior LP entry and exit from one terminal NAV snapshot, and its security specification 
requires that "new LPs receive the same marked-liability discount borne by existing LPs and do not inherit 
old trader obligations at a higher deposit NAV" (SECURITY.md#L859-L860). Opening a position carries a signed 
VPI: a skew-increasing open pays a charge, a skew-reducing open receives a rebate, and a lifetime clamp 
keeps an account's total VPI from going negative at close. 
On open, the pool's share of a positive trade cost, which includes the VPI charge, is recorded as revenue 
that has arrived in cash (CfdEngineSettlementSidecar.sol#L454-L464). A negative trade cost becomes 
poolRebatePayoutAssets (CfdEnginePlanLib.sol#L333), paid out of pool cash 
(CfdEngineSettlementSidecar.sol#L442-L444) while an equal reserve is locked in the trader's account 
(CfdEngineSettlementSidecar.sol#L466-L477). buildReconcileSnapshot builds Junior NAV from physical 
assets, trader claims and the terminal price delta (HousePoolAccountingLib.sol#L63-L71), and 
getHousePoolInputSnapshot forwards terminalLpPriceDeltaAssets unchanged 
(CfdEngineProtocolLens.sol#L70): 
uint256 terminalAssets = engineSnapshot.physicalAssets; 
uint256 terminalLiabilities = engineSnapshot.traderClaimBalanceAssets; 
int256 terminalDelta = engineSnapshot.terminalLpPriceDeltaAssets; 
if (terminalDelta >= 0) { 
terminalAssets += uint256(terminalDelta); 
} else { 
// This form also handles `type(int256).min` without negating it directly. 
terminalLiabilities += uint256(-(terminalDelta + 1)) + 1; 

At close, the clamp (CloseAccountingLib.sol#L112-L118) forces the full clawback of a paid rebate and lets 
the close return up to the full prepaid charge. 
However, neither input nets the signed vpiAccrued of open positions. NAV counts a prepaid charge as 
earned and a paid rebate as lost, although the close that settles the position reverses both, and Junior 
shares are minted and burned against that temporary NAV before the reversing close. 
A user who is both a Junior LP and a trader deposits into the Junior vault, opens a position whose VPI 
moves NAV in the chosen direction, activates or redeems shares at the mispriced NAV, then closes so the 
clamp undoes the move. The attacker buys shares cheaply or exits rich, and Junior LPs who stay bear the 
difference. No role, oracle movement or governance power is needed, but the attacker must place a 
transient Junior deposit larger than the tranche itself, plus trading margin; the same capital is reused each 
cycle and the code imposes no cap on the number of cycles. In the largest tested run on a 1M USDG Junior 
tranche, incumbents lose 30,356.96 USDG, about 3%, while the attacker gains 23,926.19 USDG over about 
15 hours; part of the incumbent loss also flows to treasury and keeper fees. A simple one-cycle variant 
costs incumbents 708.65 USDG. At the widest oracle confidence the deployment accepts (10 bps), the exit 
premium form stops paying the attacker, while the depth-amplified form still yields 9,925.75 USDG against a 
13,726.16 USDG incumbent loss. The loss is a permanent transfer out of standing Junior principal: direct 
theft of user funds, whether at-rest or in-motion, other than unclaimed yield. 

Proof of Concept

1. Deposit USDG into the Junior vault; the shares activate at the next LP epoch, priced from current NAV. 
2. Open a position whose VPI moves Junior NAV at once: a skew-increasing long prepays a charge that 
lifts NAV, or a skew-reducing short receives a rebate that lowers it. 
3. After the deposit cooldown, redeem Junior shares at the lifted NAV, or let the deposit activate at the 
lowered NAV. 
4. Close the position; the lifetime clamp refunds the charge from pool cash or claws the rebate back 
into NAV, reversing the move. 
5. Keep the share-price difference and repeat with the same capital; in the depth-amplified form, the 
deposit first raises depth for a larger long, then exits so later shorts earn oversized rebates.

Junior redemption funded in cash against an uncollected flat-short PnL pledge 
drains remaining junior LPs 

| Report ID | #97677 |
| --- | --- |
| Severity | Critical |
| Category | Smart Contract |
| Asset | https://github.com/immunefi-team/audit-comp-plether/tree/ a82d4fefb02768e5b3bddcc97dee371eee24fea8/packages/perps/src |
| Impact | Direct theft of any user funds, whether at-rest or in-motion, other than unclaimed yield |

Description

Plether Perps holds LP capital in HousePool and two TrancheVault instances; LP redemptions are requested in advance and funded in physical settlement token at a later LP epoch. The receivable/cash separation invariant (SECURITY.md#L393) states that positive marked receivables are capped by the account's PnL pledge and nettable claim, and "never increase physical redemption funding before collection".

A short filled exactly at the price cap has a zero max-profit envelope in calculateExactMaxProfit (CfdMath.sol#L76-L90), yet TerminalNavBookV2._curveInputFromEngine sets its collectible cap to pnlPledgeAssets + traderClaimBalanceAssets (TerminalNavBookV2.sol#L433-L434). For such a flat short, _encode values the position at the full pledge for any mark below its breakpoint and at zero at the cap (TerminalNavBookV2.sol#L592-L598). buildReconcileSnapshot adds that positive terminal delta to distributable assets (HousePoolAccountingLib.sol#L60-L78), while buildWithdrawalSnapshot measures free cash from physical assets only (HousePoolAccountingLib.sol#L46-L55). _settleLpEpoch reconciles first, then prices the junior redemption off the inflated juniorPrincipal:

_reconcile(accountingSnapshot, statusSnapshot); 
_checkpointEngineCarryIndexes(); 
result.cutoffEpoch = cutoffEpoch; 
RedemptionPhase memory juniorPhase; 
juniorPhase.pricingPrincipal = juniorPrincipal; 
juniorPhase.pricingSupply = _juniorShareSupply(); 
uint256 freeAssets = 
_buildWithdrawalSnapshot(accountingSnapshot, unassignedAssets, 
_pendingClaimantBucketAssets()).freeAssets; 

_fundRedemptionPhase then transfers the priced amount in cash to the vault (HousePool.sol#L1005-L1008).

However, nothing ties that cash to the pledge actually being collected. _resolveHistoricalOrderBasket prices a pending close from the unique first tick after its commit, anywhere in (commitTime, commitTime + 15s] and even when the engine already holds a newer mark (PletherOracle.sol#L610-L616), so the close 15 Immunefi Audit Competition | Immunefi / Plether

can fill at an older cap-price tick, realise zero price PnL and leave the pledge with the trader. Without an override, the engine stays in the live regime while no oracle prints exist: close orders stay pending at the `MevBoundary` check, which needs a publish time after commitment, and with open positions `settleLpEpoch` requires a mark from the current epoch. In the demonstrated case, exits and LP redemptions stalled for about seven hours, a temporary freezing of funds for at least 1 hour.

IMM-SC-INSIGHT-99916 
PletherOracle accepts anomalous single-leg Pyth quotes without per-leg bounds, 
distorting the basket mark

Description

`_computeBasketPriceFromFeeds` checks each Pyth leg only for a positive price and, if inverted, confidence below price, never against basePrices[i], so a signed price of 1 zeroes a normal leg or pushes an inverted one past CAP_PRICE, where it is clamped rather than rejected. IMM-SC-INSIGHT-100115 
CfdEngine, MarginClearinghouse and OrderRouter do not inherit the host interfaces 
their callers use 

| Report ID | #100115 |
| --- | --- |
| Severity | Insight |
| Category | Smart Contract |
| Asset | https://github.com/immunefi-team/audit-comp-plether/tree/ a82d4fefb02768e5b3bddcc97dee371eee24fea8/packages/perps/src |

Description

Sidecars, books and delegated logic call CfdEngine, MarginClearinghouse and OrderRouter through typed host interfaces such as ICfdEngineSettlementHost, IMarginClearinghouse and IOrderRouterExecutionHost, but the inheritance lists never declare them, so the compiler does not keep signatures aligned.
